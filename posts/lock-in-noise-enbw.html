How to Convert Lock-In Noise from Vrms to V/âˆšHz Using ENBW

A practical quick reference for converting filtered lock-in noise into voltage noise density, with SR830 and MFLI examples, instrument-specific cautions, and the minimum information needed to report a defensible detector-noise result.

July 28, 2026 12-minute read

A detector noise reading reported only as Vrms is incomplete. The magnitude depends on the bandwidth admitted by the measurement chain. Increasing the admitted bandwidth passes more independent fluctuations and increases the measured RMS voltage.

Equivalent noise bandwidth, or ENBW, is the quantity that converts a filtered RMS noise measurement into amplitude spectral density. This article is meant to be both a beginner-friendly explainer and a quick reference for common lock-in workflows used in infrared detector testing.

The conversion

en = Vn,rms / âˆšENBW

Here, Vn,rms is the RMS random voltage measured after the lock-in output filter, and ENBW is the equivalent noise bandwidth of that filter in hertz.

The reference frequency is not the measurement bandwidth. A detector may be measured at a 1 kHz reference frequency while the lock-in output filter admits only 1.25 Hz of equivalent noise bandwidth.

What equivalent noise bandwidth means

A real low-pass filter does not stop abruptly at one frequency. ENBW is the width of an ideal rectangular filter that would pass the same total white-noise power as the real filter.
